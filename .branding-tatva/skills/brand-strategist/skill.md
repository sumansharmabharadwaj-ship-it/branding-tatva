# Brand Strategist — Branding Tatva

## The five elements are the explanatory device, not the product

Earth (foundation/positioning), Water (experience/customer journey), Fire (expression/attention), Air (voice/narrative), Space (presence/recognition). They organize the site's storytelling. Brand strategy — positioning, distinctiveness, mental availability, category design, semiotics, cultural relevance, brand architecture — is what's actually being sold. A section should never exist just to showcase the five-element framework; it should use the framework to make a specific branding claim land.

## Real proof only

Every number on the site traces back to `src/data/projects.ts` — five real client engagements (Dr. Haley Nutrition, MyShopInEurope, Executive Springboard, HerbalCart, Plaxonic), each with a verified `outcome`/`stats` field. Never invent a testimonial, a client logo, or a metric. If a section wants proof and none exists yet for that specific claim, say less rather than fabricate — this "commercial honesty" rule is load-bearing across the whole site and has been enforced consistently every time it came up.

## The founder's actual credibility

M.A. Clinical Psychology + B.A. (Hons) English Literature — "one studies how people notice and decide, the other studies how language carries meaning." This is the real, specific trust hook (see Home's Trust beat), not a generic "years of experience" claim. Lean on it directly rather than reaching for agency-credibility language (awards, team size, client logos that don't exist).

## Sequencing a page's argument

The established sequence for a page that needs to build a case (see Home): Attention → Curiosity → Trust → Authority → Proof → soft CTA → deeper explanation → hard CTA. Trust has to land before Authority — naming the framework before establishing why the person naming it should be believed reads as hollow.

## Vocabulary discipline

Branding vocabulary (positioning, distinctive assets, mental availability, category design, brand salience, cultural relevance, brand architecture, verbal identity, semiotics) over marketing vocabulary (growth, engagement, reach, visibility, performance, ROI) whenever a sentence is making the actual strategic point. Marketing-vocabulary words aren't banned outright, but they should never be the load-bearing word in a claim about what branding does.
