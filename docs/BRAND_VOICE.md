# Branding Tatva — Voice System

## Grounding
This voice is built from two real things about Suman, not aspirational branding language: a clinical psychology background (attention, perception, behaviour) and an English literature background (language, narrative, meaning) — both actually applied, across real client work, to content and brand strategy. The voice should sound like that combination: someone who notices how people actually read, scroll, decide, and remember, and who also just writes well.

## First-person rule
"I" when Suman speaks about her thinking, work, or process. "Branding Tatva" for the service system itself. No "we" — this is a personal practice, not implied as a bigger team.

## Seven voice principles

1. **Grounded clarity** — explain branding in terms a first-time founder understands. If a sentence sounds impressive but doesn't tell the reader anything they can act on, cut it.
2. **Human observation** — let the psychology background show through specific, real observations about attention and behaviour, not vague claims of "expertise." Example: not "I understand your audience deeply," but "People decide whether to keep reading in about the same time it takes to notice a doorway is open."
3. **Quiet confidence** — state what Suman does plainly. No exaggeration, no "the best," no self-congratulation.
4. **Elemental thinking, used sparingly** — earth/water/fire/air/space language should appear where it clarifies a structure (like the Services page), not in every sentence. One elemental reference per section, maximum.
5. **Friendly precision** — accessible language, but specific. "I'll help you find your positioning" is vague; "I'll help you answer the one question most brand pages dodge — who this isn't for" is precise.
6. **Commercial honesty** — talk about clarity, recognition, trust, and attention. Never promise sales, followers, or revenue. The Dr. Haley Nutrition numbers can be shown as *what happened*, not as a guarantee of what will happen for a new client.
7. **Cultural intelligence** — Indian references (Tatva, elemental system) used with restraint and real meaning, never as decoration or cliché.

## Sentence-level habits
- Vary sentence length. Short sentences do the work of emphasis.
- Headlines: concise and plain, not poetic for its own sake.
- Body copy: conversational, but edited — this is a copywriter's website, so the writing itself has to be proof of the service.
- CTAs: inviting, never pushy. "Start a brand conversation" over "Get Started Now."
- Microcopy (form hints, errors): kind and specific. "That doesn't look like a working email — mind checking it?" not "Invalid input."

## Phrases this site will never use
We bring ideas to life · Where creativity meets strategy · Your success is our success · Transform your vision · Unlock your potential · Stand out from the crowd · Take your brand to the next level · One-stop solution · Tailored solutions · In today's digital landscape · Elevate your online presence · Cutting edge · Game-changing · Seamless solutions · Meaningful connections · Crafting experiences · Passion meets purpose · Make an impact · Turn dreams into reality · A unique blend · Results-driven solutions · Your trusted partner · We believe in · At the heart of · In a world where · More than just · Not just · Whether you are · From concept to creation

Also rationed (used only when they add real meaning, not as filler): brand, powerful, unique, impactful, creative, strategy, meaningful, elevate, transform, journey.

## What service copy must always answer
1. The situation the client is in
2. The underlying branding problem (which element is weak or missing)
3. What Suman will actually do
4. What the client receives
5. What can realistically improve as a result
6. The next step

## Two short examples (not final copy — tone reference only)

**Instead of:** "We craft powerful brand experiences that elevate your business to the next level."
**Write:** "Most brands don't fail because the logo is wrong. They fail because the parts don't agree with each other — the website sounds confident, the Instagram sounds unsure, and no one can say in one sentence what the business actually does. I find where that disagreement is, and fix it."

**Instead of:** "Whether you're just starting out or looking to elevate an existing brand, I'm here to help."
**Write:** "If you're starting from an idea, I help you find the shape before you spend money on the wrong version of it. If you already have a business but it's stopped being memorable, I help you find out why — usually it's simpler than people expect."
