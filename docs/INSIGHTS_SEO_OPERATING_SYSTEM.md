# Insights SEO operating system

Status: launch-ready foundation, followed by a 90-day evidence cycle
Owner: Suman Sharma
Canonical editorial path: `/insights`

## Search objective

The Insights library should earn qualified discovery around brand strategy questions asked by founders and service businesses, then move the reader naturally toward a useful next decision. Rankings are an outcome of relevance, quality, authority, technical accessibility, and sustained maintenance. They are never treated as guaranteed inventory.

The first commercial wedge is deliberately narrow:

1. brand positioning strategy for service businesses
2. brand audit checklist before a rebrand
3. brand awareness versus brand recall
4. brand messaging framework
5. brand strategy framework

These pages form the initial authority cluster. Each article owns a distinct intent and links into the next useful question rather than competing for the same query.

## Page contract

Every new article must contain:

- one clearly defined primary search intent
- a title that matches the reader's real question
- a direct answer near the beginning
- visible authorship and an accurate published or updated date
- three to five useful takeaways
- one original working framework, diagnostic, checklist, or decision tool
- substantial explanation written from direct brand practice and observation
- examples or evidence where the claim needs support
- a useful FAQ section based on real follow-up questions
- at least three contextual internal links
- one relevant commercial next step
- a unique meta title, description, canonical URL, and social image
- inclusion in the sitemap, topic hub, RSS feed, and `llms.txt` when the guide is strategically important

No article should exist only to occupy a keyword. If the piece cannot add a useful distinction, framework, example, or decision, it should remain unpublished.

## Internal linking rules

Every article should link:

- upward to its topic hub
- sideways to two or three related guides
- backward to the foundation decision it depends on
- forward to the decision a reader is likely to face next
- commercially only where the service genuinely resolves the question

Anchor text should describe the destination. Avoid repeated generic anchors such as `read more` or `click here`.

Whenever a new article is published, update at least two older articles with contextual links to it. This keeps the library connected and gives the new page established crawl paths.

## Evidence and citation rules

Use first-party evidence wherever possible:

- anonymised client observations
- documented process decisions
- before-and-after message architecture
- screenshots or diagrams created for the article
- verified outcomes with clear context
- customer language collected during discovery or delivery

Use reputable external sources when a claim depends on research, law, platform behaviour, or a named theory. Prefer original research, official documentation, books, academic papers, and primary datasets. Avoid decorative citation piles.

Every factual claim should be traceable. Every opinion should be clearly framed as analysis or practice-based judgement.

## Search Console launch sequence

After the branch reaches production:

1. Verify that `/insights`, every article, and every topic hub return HTTP 200.
2. Confirm the old `/blog` routes return permanent redirects to the intended new destinations.
3. Submit `https://brandingtatva.com/sitemap.xml` in Google Search Console.
4. Inspect the Insights archive and five pillar URLs individually.
5. Request indexing for the archive and pillars.
6. Confirm the canonical URL selected by Google matches the declared canonical.
7. Monitor indexing, enhancement reports, crawl errors, and Core Web Vitals.
8. Record the first date each page appears for impressions.

## 30, 60, and 90-day review

### Days 1 to 30

Measure:
- indexed versus discovered URLs
- impressions by query and page
- unexpected queries that reveal new language
- mobile usability and Core Web Vitals
- click-through rate on queries already reaching positions 5 to 20

Actions:
- repair indexing or canonical issues
- rewrite titles and descriptions where impressions arrive without clicks
- add links from relevant service and work pages
- publish the first three supporting articles

### Days 31 to 60

Measure:
- query clusters forming around each pillar
- pages moving into positions 8 to 30
- article paths that lead to service or contact engagement
- terms where Google ranks the wrong page

Actions:
- resolve cannibalisation through clearer intent, headings, and internal links
- add examples, diagrams, or missing subtopics to pages already earning impressions
- publish supporting articles four through seven
- seek relevant citations, partnerships, interviews, and editorial mentions

### Days 61 to 90

Measure:
- first-page entries
- movement of supporting pages and their pillar
- branded versus non-branded discovery
- returning readers and newsletter conversion
- enquiries influenced by Insights sessions

Actions:
- refresh pages that are close to first-page thresholds
- expand sections that match real search queries
- consolidate any weak overlap
- publish supporting articles eight through ten
- choose the next cluster using evidence rather than intuition

## Monthly refresh rule

Every month, select pages using this order:

1. high impressions, low click-through rate
2. positions 8 to 20 with strong commercial relevance
3. pages ranking for an adjacent intent they do not yet answer well
4. pages with outdated evidence or missing examples
5. orphaned or weakly linked pages

Refresh meaningfully. Updating a date without improving the page damages trust and provides no editorial value.

## Ranking scorecard

Track each target query and page with:

- intended search intent
- current ranking URL
- average position
- impressions
- clicks
- click-through rate
- indexed status
- number of contextual internal links
- number and quality of external referring pages
- last substantive update
- assisted enquiry or newsletter conversion

The primary success measure is qualified organic discovery that leads to useful reading and relevant enquiries. Ranking position is one signal inside that larger system.
