# Hero Headline Directions

Six real options, evaluated on the criteria the project calls for: clarity, originality, emotional recall, brand relevance, conversion potential, SEO, and mobile readability. None of these are locked in — flag which one(s) you want to test or revise.

---

### A. "Every memorable brand begins with the right elements."
*(currently live)*
- Clarity: high — instantly parseable
- Originality: medium — "elements" ties to Tatva without over-explaining it
- Emotional recall: medium
- SEO: weak — no searchable phrase
- Mobile: fits two lines cleanly
- **Read:** Safe, warm, slightly soft. Good as a fallback; doesn't yet earn its place as *the* signature line.

### B. "Most brands are visible. Very few are remembered."
- Clarity: high
- Originality: high — a genuine contrast structure, not a generic claim
- Emotional recall: high — names a feeling most business owners already have
- SEO: weak on its own, but pairs well with a subhead carrying "brand strategist" / "brand recognition"
- Mobile: reads well broken across two short lines
- **Read:** The strongest emotional hook of the six. Doubles as the natural headline for the "Attention Problem" section even if not used in the hero.

### C. "Find the elements your brand hasn't found yet."
- Clarity: high
- Originality: medium-high — active, benefit-led framing of "Tatva"
- Emotional recall: medium
- SEO: medium — "brand elements" has real search volume
- Mobile: clean
- **Read:** More direct than A, still on-concept. Good middle option.

### D. "A brand holds together when its elements agree — not before."
- Clarity: medium — requires a beat to parse "agree"
- Originality: high — memorable structure, sounds like something Suman would actually say
- Emotional recall: medium-high
- SEO: weak
- Mobile: slightly long, wraps to three lines on small screens
- **Read:** The most "voice-forward" option — sounds like a person talking, not a tagline. Risk: the parenthetical clause may not survive translation to a large display font well.

### E. "The elements of a brand people actually remember."
- Clarity: high
- Originality: medium
- Emotional recall: medium-high
- SEO: medium — "brand" + "remember" is a reasonable long-tail combination
- Mobile: clean, two lines
- **Read:** Plainest of the group. Strong fallback if user testing shows "Tatva" needs more explanation than the poetic options give it.

### F. "I look at how attention actually works, then build a brand around it."
- Clarity: high
- Originality: high — leans directly on the psychology differentiator, first-person
- Emotional recall: medium
- SEO: weak
- Mobile: long — needs a smaller display size or a line break
- **Read:** The most differentiated versus a generic branding agency. Better suited to the About page opening (where it's already doing similar work) than the hero, to avoid redundancy.

---

## Recommendation
Lead the hero with **B**, since it's the strongest combination of originality and emotional recall, and it sets up the rest of the page's logic (attention → elements → work). Keep **A** as the supporting line directly beneath it, since it explains *what to do about* the problem B names. This pairing gives the hero both a hook and a resolution in two lines, rather than one line trying to do both jobs.

If you'd rather lead with clarity over emotional punch, **C** is the safer swap for the primary headline.
