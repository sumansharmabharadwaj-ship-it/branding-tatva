## 2. Positioning — five directions to choose from

1. **The Elements Approach**
 *"Branding Tatva finds the elements that make a business worth noticing, and shapes them into a brand people recognise, trust, and remember."*
 Leans fully into the Tatva concept. Most distinctive, most on-brand, slightly more abstract until explained.

2. **The Clarity Approach**
 *"I help businesses say what they mean — clearly enough that people remember it, and consistently enough that they trust it."*
 Plainest, most immediately understandable. Strong for SEO and first-time visitors who don't yet know what "Tatva" means.

3. **The Behavioural Approach**
 *"Branding is a question of attention and memory. I study how people notice, trust, and remember — then build brands around that."*
 Leans on the psychology background as a genuine differentiator, not decoration. Distinctive versus generic branding agencies.

4. **The Range Approach**
 *"From a first-time founder to an existing brand that's lost its shape, I find the story worth telling and the system that keeps telling it."*
 Leans on the "new brands / existing brands" dual audience directly in the positioning line itself.

5. **The Craft Approach**
 *"Good branding isn't a mood board. It's positioning, voice, and consistency working together — and I build all three."*
 Most direct rebuttal to "logo-only design studio" positioning; useful if that's a real competitive confusion Suman runs into.

**My recommendation:** a blend of #1 and #3 — lead with the Elements framing for identity and memorability, but let the psychology/attention angle do the differentiation work in supporting copy. #2 is the strongest fallback if user testing shows "Tatva" needs more explanation than expected.

I'll hold final selection until you weigh in — this is a launch-defining decision, not one I should lock in alone.
